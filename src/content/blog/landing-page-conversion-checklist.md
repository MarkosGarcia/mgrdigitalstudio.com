Most landing pages convert somewhere between 2–4% of visitors. If yours is in that range, it's not broken — but it's also leaving a lot on the table, especially if you're paying for traffic. A one-point improvement in conversion rate can mean meaningfully more leads without spending another dollar on ads.

Here are seven changes worth checking first. None of them require a redesign.

## 1. Put the form above the fold

If a visitor has to scroll to find your contact or sign-up form, a large share of them simply won't. Most people arrive already knowing why they clicked — they don't need a long story before they can act. Move the form (or a clear, obvious way to reach it) into the first screen they see, on both desktop and mobile.

## 2. Say exactly what happens next

Vague calls to action like "Get Started" or "Learn More" don't tell visitors anything. Replace them with a specific, benefit-driven headline — under about seven words — that states the outcome and the format: a checklist, a call, a quote, a demo. People scan pages rather than read them, so clarity has to win in a glance.

## 3. Test the form on an actual phone

A form that looks fine on desktop can be frustrating on mobile: cramped fields, tiny buttons, awkward taps. Give inputs enough padding, make buttons large enough to tap comfortably, and actually fill out the form yourself on a real device before assuming it works.

## 4. Embed the form instead of using a pop-up

Every extra step — closing an overlay, waiting for a widget to load — gives visitors a chance to leave. On a page built around one specific action, put the form directly on the page instead of behind a pop-up.

## 5. Ask for less, and enable autofill

Every additional field is a reason to abandon the form. If you don't need a phone number to deliver what you're offering, don't ask for it yet — you can follow up later once there's already trust. Pairing a short form with proper autofill attributes (name, email) removes friction, especially on mobile.

## 6. Add a short, specific testimonial near the form

A single line of real social proof — a name, a specific result, ideally a photo — placed right next to the form reassures visitors who are on the fence. Specific beats generic: "helped us reply to leads twice as fast" says more than "great service."

## 7. Give the thank-you page a next step

Once someone submits a form, they're paying attention. Rather than a plain "thanks for reaching out" message, offer one clear next step: booking a short call, downloading something related, or a small next action tied to what they just requested.
